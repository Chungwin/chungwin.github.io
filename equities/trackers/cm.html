How to read this file — critical minerals

Same method as the copper and precious-metals tabs

  • 2nd 3×? (the chip on each card): after a first 3× in years 1–5, is there room for another 3× by about year 10? Yes = a believable path (big or growing resource, several assets, growth paid from cash flow); partly = capped or dependent on the metal price; little = the first re-rating is most of the value. Judged per share at today's market value. It is not about cash: months of cash left are on the card's Financials tab (where it exists) and in the funding line. Full explanation on the rankings page under table B.
  • Financials (tab on the card, pilot on 5 companies so far): the last 8 quarters and 3 fiscal years read from the original financial statements on the company's own website (no data feed), simplified to the lines that matter — cash, burn, months of cash left, what went into the ground vs overhead, money raised and share count, and for producers revenue, margin and free cash flow. Every column links to the PDF it came from; every quarter is checked (opening cash + net flow = closing cash). The findings feed the runway and risk notes; there is no separate score.
  • Management (chip on each card, full review in the card's Management tab): the people graded A–D on track record (what they built, sold or ran before, misses included), fit (have they done this exact job: same stage, deposit type, region) and candour (a dated promises-vs-delivery log). A★ tier 1 · B solid, or unproven but clean — the default · C real weaknesses · D a pattern of failing shareholders. Neutral by default: the odds move only with evidence, full weight for explorers and developers, half for producers. A fourth score, skin in the game (1–5, in the Management tab), is the team's own money at risk — a conviction signal that never moves the odds but breaks near-ties in list B; a team that gets paid while shareholders lose scores 1. Reviewed 5 Oct 2026, refreshed every Saturday.
  • Bucket (the colour bar): cheap defined resources needs at least an Indicated resource plus a study; grade / discovery is drill-bit optionality; quality / jurisdiction is de-risked and priced for it; scale & financing is a big asset whose gate is the cheque; producer / cash flow holds names with revenue, valued on EV/EBITDA and margin. Processor / midstream (critical minerals only) is a company whose case is a separation, refining or conversion plant rather than a deposit: it has no resource to value, so the anchor is EV per tpa of capacity, the contracted share of capacity, and margin per kg, and the risk screen is technology scale-up, feed security, offtake/price and funding. Its rankings columns use EV/EBITDA on run-rate EBITDA at each price case where a defensible figure exists.
  • Gate, differentiator, Funding & next catalyst panel (funded / partly / not funded to the next dated event), risk 1–5 (chance of permanent loss or no re-rate inside 3–5 years) and the Rule screen (after-tax IRR ≥ 25–30% at a conservative price, NPV ≥ 2–3× capex, payback ≲ 3 years) mean exactly what they mean on the other two tabs.
  • Shareholder structure is shown, never scored. 💸 Pick is a personal flag with an entry plan; picks don't enter rankings. News links to the company's own releases.
  • Filing rule: a company sits where its primary investment case sits. Aftermath (manganese), Rua Gold (antimony) and Osisko Metals' critical-minerals spin-out stay on their current tabs.

What changes for critical minerals

  • Mineral group badge beside each name — Battery (Li, Ni, graphite, Co, Mn), Defence / strategic (Sb, W, rare earths, Ga/Ge, Sc), Uranium — with the specific mineral as the badge text and a matching filter row.
  • The anchor depends on the mineral: lithium — EV per tonne LCE by confidence category, and which product (spodumene concentrate vs carbonate/hydroxide); uranium — EV per lb U3O8 by category, and term vs spot price; graphite, nickel, cobalt — EV per tonne of contained product, read against whether the product is qualified with a battery-maker; rare earths — NdPr (and Dy/Tb) content, because the basket price is mostly magnet metals; antimony, tungsten, Ga/Ge — there is often no transparent price, so the anchor is the offtake contract.
  • Policy is a price input, not background. China dominates processing for most of these minerals and has used export controls on gallium, germanium, antimony and rare earths. Western price floors, government offtakes and agency funding (US DoD/DPA, EXIM, Canada, the EU Critical Raw Materials Act) can decide both the price a project gets and how it is financed. Every card states which policy lever it depends on and how durable that lever is.
  • Processing is often the real gate. A resource is not a product: battery-grade qualification, a separation circuit (rare earths) or a conversion plant (lithium hydroxide) can cost more and take longer than the mine. The gate names which step is missing.
  • Uranium reads differently: utilities buy on long-term contracts, so a producer's contract book (volumes, floors, ceilings) matters as much as spot; ISR vs conventional mining decides capex and timing; enrichment and conversion are separate bottlenecks.

Research shorthand, not investment advice. Prices for many of these minerals are thin, opaque or policy-set — read anchors as shapes, not decimal-precise rankings. Rankings: Critical minerals tab on the rankings page — certainty vs multi-bagger, grows with this tab.

Thesis

Ucore is a pre-commercial, merchant rare-earth separator. It owns RapidSX, a compact column-based solvent-extraction platform (conventional SX chemistry in faster, smaller contactors — claimed up to 60% smaller footprint), proven on a 52-stage demo plant in Kingston, Ontario: 5,726 operating hours and ~1.8 t of REO-equivalent processed over seven separations to Jan 2026, 7,400+ hours of simulated runtime, 99.5% NdPr from two feed types and 99.9% Dy with a conventional polish. It is building the Louisiana Strategic Metals Complex in a brownfield building at England Airpark, Alexandria. A May 2026 engineering re-base (Orbital) set the plan as Machine A (≈600 tpa TREO, US$60M, commissioning H1 2027) then Production Line 1 (≈3,000 tpa NdPr, US$44M + a US$31M oxide/packaging block, undated, unfunded), ≈9,600 tpa at full build. Capex for the first 3,600 tpa doubled from US$65M to US$135M while first-phase capacity shrank from 2,000 tpa (2026) to 600 tpa (2027).

Funding: War Department awards US$27.0M (only US$1.9M of Phase 2 claimed by 30 Jun), Canadian C$4.28M plus a conditional C$36.3M, up to US$15M of Louisiana incentives; C$69M raised at C$2.80 in August, director loans repaid in September — est. ~C$75M cash, no debt, 152.5M shares (161M fully diluted, +31% in four months). Commercially nothing is binding: 0% of capacity contracted for feed or for sales.

At C$2.59 (US$278M; EV ~US$226M) the market pays US$63k per tpa of costed capacity against a US$37.5k/tpa build cost, and on my illustrative merchant-separator assumptions the 3,600 tpa build earns ~US$12M EBITDA at today's ex-China NdPr — the market cap is 15–40× that. The bull case is heavy-REE scarcity (Dy/Tb at ~5× China while the April-2025 controls hold) plus a US-policy champion premium or a strategic deal; the bear case is a technology/feed/offtake triple unknown, a two-year slip history and plant economics that don't cover the valuation. Stock −53% YTD, −80% from the C$13.07 high of late 2025, below the August deal price.

Product, price & policy lever

Product & processing. Separated rare-earth oxides: NdPr oxide (the volume product), plus Dy, Tb, Sm and Gd from heavy feeds (the value products), from a merchant separation plant that buys mixed concentrate (MREC/MREO) from third parties. Kingston demo output so far: 99.5% NdPr from ~2 t of Vietnamese ionic-clay MREO and ~5 t of North American bastnaesite MREC (Jun–Aug 2026), and 99.9% Dy, which needed a conventional solvent-extraction polish after the RapidSX circuit (Jul 2026). Samples have gone to unnamed Western, Japanese and Korean magnet makers; nothing has passed qualification, and formal qualification trials are scheduled for H1 2027 on Machine A output. Not yet proven: continuous commercial-scale RapidSX (Machine A ≈600 tpa is roughly 25–30× the demo's implied annual rate) and the US$31M oxide production and packaging block, which is not built.

Price. Opaque and split by geography. Ex-China NdPr oxide ~US$95/kg CIF (Benchmark, 21 Aug 2026), below China's domestic ~US$107.5/kg: the Western NdPr premium has inverted on ex-China oversupply, and is ~30% off the late-April peak. The heavies are the opposite: Dy ~US$205/kg in China vs US$1,100–2,000+ ex-China, Tb ~US$990 vs US$3,600–5,000+ (REEx, Aug 2026), because China's April-2025 licensing on Sm, Gd, Tb and Dy is still in force. Prices are assessed by price-reporting agencies (Benchmark, Fastmarkets, SMM) and heavy-REE trade is bilateral and confidential. The US$110/kg NdPr floor applies to MP Materials only; Serra Verde–USA Rare Earth's floors (Dy US$575, Tb US$2,050) show what a Western heavy-REE contract can look like. S&P puts long-run NdPr cost at US$75–80/kg.

Policy lever. The case is a policy trade. US Department of War (formerly DoD) other-transaction awards total US$27.0M: Phase 1 US$4.0M, Phase 2 US$18.4M (Jun 2025) plus a US$4.6M Sm/Gd top-up (14 Sep 2026); only US$1.9M of Phase 2 had been claimed by 30 Jun, with 15 of 20 milestones outstanding. DPAS priority rating (Sep 2025). Canada: C$4.28M NRCan contribution plus a conditional C$36.3M package for a Kingston Sm/Gd plant, no definitive agreement yet. Louisiana: up to US$15M of mostly tax- and performance-based incentives. No DOE loan, EXIM letter or DPA Title III equity, unlike Aclara (EXIM LOI up to US$750M, Sep 2026) or MP (DoD equity, loan and floor, Jul 2025). China: the April-2025 heavy-REE licensing holds; the suspension of the October-2025 expanded controls was due to expire 10 Nov 2026 and now runs to 10 Jan 2027 with the US–China truce. Durability: the award money is milestone-based and small against US$135M of capex, and the heavy-REE premium Ucore targets exists only while China's controls bite. A US–China settlement is the main policy downside.

Valuation Anchor

Processor anchor: EV per tpa of capacity, contracted share, margin per kg. EV ~US$226M = US$63k per tpa of the costed 3,600 tpa build, 1.7× what it costs to build; contracted share 0%; margin per kg not guided (illustratively US$2–5/kg TREO). On those numbers there is no starting discount — the market is paying for technology, policy and strategic optionality, not for plant cash flow.

What would change that: (1) feed bought at ≤60% payable, or (2) a sales contract with a floor like MP's or Serra Verde's — each worth US$5–10M of EBITDA at today's price; (3) China's heavy-REE controls hardening after 10 Nov, which lifts the Dy/Tb credits Machine A is configured for; (4) a government or strategic transaction that values RapidSX as a platform rather than one plant.

Two lenses on the upside. The numbers above are a merchant model. If separation is the chokepoint, a working separator keeps more of the value: on heavy-rich clay feed (illustratively ~4% Dy, ~0.7% Tb — a basket of ~US$85–90/kg TREO at Western prices vs ~US$27 for light feed) bought at a 50% payable, plus Line 1 at 60%, illustrative EBITDA is ~US$33M (~0.8× at today's prices, ~2× at US$180 NdPr). And a working Western heavy-REE separator would be priced as a scarce strategic asset, not on EBITDA: ~3–5× if Machine A runs and an offtake is binding. My odds of that 3× holding: ~18–20% (Machine A + a binding contract by end-2027 ~35–40% × ~50%).

For completeness: against Bokan's M&I (~24 kt TREO) the EV is ~US$9,300/t TREO — not meaningful, since Bokan is deferred to the late 2020s and not in the plan.

  2. The scarce elements: Dy, Tb, Sm and Gd, where ex-China prices are ~5× China's — Sm/Gd for defence magnets is a niche few Western players target.
  3. Feed-agnostic: demonstrated on ionic-clay MREO and bastnaesite MREC, with MOUs across Australia, Brazil, Greenland, Canada and Thailand.
  4. Modular, low-capex brownfield next to US magnet makers (Vulcan, VAC/eVAC) that are themselves government-backed; clean balance sheet after September.

Counterweight: RapidSX is still SX chemistry — the edge is footprint and speed, not a new separation principle, and the Dy product needed a conventional finish. Four-plus years of MOUs have produced no binding feed or offtake, and VAC's window lapsed. First production slipped two years while capex doubled. A merchant separator earns only the separation margin, set by feed terms. Competitors with their own feed or bigger government cheques (MP, Lynas, Energy Fuels, Aclara) are ahead.

The Gate

Turning the RIB North and Lac 50 West radiometric hits into a compliant, assayed resource large enough (>100 Mlb at >0.3% U₃O₈) to justify a mine in Nunavut — proving the 'district' is more than wide low-grade envelopes with sporadic centimetre-scale high grade.

The 2026 program delivered what an explorer promises — 16 step-outs, 14 mineralised, a horizon traced from 300 m to 1.45 km, a new discovery 4 km from the deposit — and every one of those intercepts is a scintillometer reading. 'Composite' intervals sum many thin zones across 100–200 m of core (RIBN-DD-011's 20 m sits between 494 and 700 m). The assays in Q4 are the first hard test; the resource is 2027–28; the mine, if ever, is a decade and a Nunavut review away.

(3) Nunavut's stance on uranium — the fragile lever: NIRB rejected Kiggavik in 2015–16; NTI's 2007 policy allows uranium mining with conditions. No DoD/DPA/EXIM money and none expected for a Canadian explorer.

Jurisdiction risk

Canada, Tier-1 — Nunavut, with uranium-specific caveats. Lac 50 sits on Inuit Owned Land (NTI holds surface and subsurface; an exploration agreement is in place, royalty terms undisclosed); Kivalliq Inuit Association and CIRNAC land-use permits run February–October. A mine means a Nunavut Impact Review Board assessment, a Nunavut Water Board licence and a CNSC uranium-mine licence. The precedent is Kiggavik: Areva's project near Baker Lake was rejected by NIRB in 2015–16 (no start date, community opposition). NTI's 2007 policy permits uranium mining with conditions. Infrastructure: none — a fly-in camp with a gravel strip, 225 km from Baker Lake; Agnico's Meliadine and Meadowbank show Nunavut mines get built, at high capex and with Inuit impact-and-benefit agreements. The CEO calls the CNSC 'an advantage, not a bottleneck'; the fragile lever is the territorial social licence, not the federal regulator.

Saskatchewan (Gemini/GMZ, Pasfield, Spire/Horizon) is the world's best uranium jurisdiction and ATHA's ground there has had no reported 2025–26 fieldwork. Labrador (Central Mineral Belt, ~250,000 acres) has a uranium mining history and a moratorium lifted in 2012. The policy backdrop — the US Prohibiting Russian Uranium Imports Act with waivers expiring by 1 Jan 2028, Russia still ~26% of US enrichment purchases in 2025 — tightens Western demand into exactly the window a Nunavut resource would be defined.

Product, price & policy lever

Product & processing. A ~30–32% Ni sulphide flotation concentrate (conventional crush-grind-float at 3.5 Mtpa) — not a battery chemical: the sulphate refinery was shelved in Mar 2024 and will return only 'when and if market conditions allow'. 20 kt a year is sold to Glencore for Sudbury on LME-linked terms with payability that flexes with price (80% base); the other ~70% is unallocated and is meant to come with strategic equity. The flowsheet (30% concentrate at 70% recovery, 1.8% mass pull) is testwork-level with no pilot plant announced — the one technical step not yet proven.

Price. LME nickel ~US$15,830/t on 1 Oct 2026, −6% on the month, near 39-week lows, with ~279 kt of LME stocks; the price is exchange-transparent, but two-thirds of supply is Indonesian NPI/MHP priced off Chinese benchmarks. The study deck (US$19,800) is 25% above spot; the company's own spot case is US$15,500. There is no quoted 'Class 1 premium' — it shows up as concentrate payability and smelter appetite, and Western smelters are short of sulphide feed. The bull argument is Indonesian quota cuts and Morowali water constraints; the bear is the LME overhang.

Policy lever. Brazilian, not Western: BNDES (the state development bank) in the debt stack with a R$1bn LOI, SUDAM's 15.25% tax rate and Pará's state incentives (granted Jun 2026). 'Non-Indonesian, low-carbon Class-1 feed' is the pitch to Western, Japanese and Korean buyers and export-credit agencies, but no DoD/DPA, EXIM, Canadian or EU money has been announced. All levers are letters of intent or tax rates; nothing has arrived as cash. Durable (BNDES and SUDAM are long-standing), but not the kind of policy that pays for a mine.

Differentiator

The scarce end of the nickel market: a high-grade (30% Ni), low-impurity sulphide concentrate that Western smelters short of feed want — the opposite of Indonesian NPI/MHP — with Glencore's Sudbury as the first buyer.

  1. Permits complete in a country where that is hard — LP, LI, mining lease, grid, tax incentives all in hand; few nickel developers anywhere are at this stage.
  2. Low capital intensity — US$380M for 22.6 ktpa, 80% renewable grid power, a 15% tax rate.
  3. Payback 1.8 years and ±US$216M NPV per 10% in nickel — leveraged exposure if Indonesian quotas bite.
  4. A state bank in the debt stack and a management team with twenty years in Carajás.

Counterweight: 'months from FID' since mid-2025; Glencore's condition has lapsed; the equity gap is the market cap; AISC on a payable basis (US$4.43/lb) is mid-curve, not bottom-quartile, and payability flexes down with price; the sulphate option was shelved; eight employees run a US$380M build on the Amazon fringe. Thesis

A land-and-targets story in the right postcode. Moonlite (~1,700 km² / 430,764 acres; 67,613 ha on the project page) sits in the south-western Athabasca Basin — the Patterson Lake corridor that produced NexGen's Arrow (256.7 Mlb M+I), Fission's Triple R (114.9 Mlb Indicated), Orano's Shea Creek and F3's JR zone. The package is a mix of 100%-owned claims and 47 claims (547,524 acres at signing) under option from ATHA Energy: Stallion is sole operator and earns 70% by spending C$12M of Saskatchewan exploration-expenditure credits within 60 months of the July 2023 definitive agreement (C$3.31M in the first year, the rest by ~July 2028); ATHA keeps 30% including a legacy carried interest. The package has shrunk from 715,450 acres in 2023 to 430,764 — which claims were dropped is not disclosed.

The flagship target is Coyote: an 8.5 km corridor on 100%-owned ground with nine ground-EM conductors (six above 10 S), a gravity low the company calls 'one of the strongest ground gravity anomalies in the Athabasca Basin', basement structure at NE/NW intersections, unconformity at ~450 m. It had never been drilled. The maiden program (Feb 2026, two rigs, Base Drilling) was planned at 4,000 m, expanded to 5,500 m in May and 6,750 m in August; 4,585 m were done by 29 Sep. Every hole hit wide fault zones, quartz dissolution, brecciation, clay and graphitic faulting — ML26-001A two fault zones of 66–109 m — and 'elevated radioactivity' above 300 cps. The best so far: ML26-005, 2.3 m averaging 595 cps (max 1,416 cps) at 572–574 m, plus 0.3 m at 648 cps; ML26-006A 0.3 m at 458 cps. For scale, ore-grade Athabasca intercepts run tens of thousands of cps. No assay from any 2026 hole has been published; samples went to SRC in May. Two holes were lost (ML26-002, -002A); a serious injury to two contractors in April paused the program for three weeks.

Second target Stone Island (100%-owned, 18 km conductive trend): 676 line-km of VTEM (Feb 2026) and 519 gravity stations (summer) produced three target areas, A being the strongest; untested. How to read a card. The bucket colour on the left is the archetype. The gate is the single thing that has to go right; the differentiator is what the company has that peers don't. For critical minerals, also read which processing step is missing and which policy lever the price depends on.

Many of these markets are thin, opaque or set by policy; resource anchors use different units by mineral (t LCE, lb U3O8, t contained graphite, kg NdPr). This is research shorthand, not investment advice.
