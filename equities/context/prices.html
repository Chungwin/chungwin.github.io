Correlation table and how to read it
How closely they moved togetherValueWhat it means

Correlation compares daily moves: +1 means they moved in the same direction every day, 0 means no relation, −1 means opposite directions. The COPX ratio says how many percent the miners typically moved for each 1% move in copper. Copper is the LME official cash settlement (Westmetall, US$ per tonne), fixed around midday London; the S&P 500 is the index close in New York (FMP); COPX is the closing price of the Global X Copper Miners ETF (Nasdaq historical data, checked against stockanalysis.com and digrin.com). All are price only, without dividends. LME copper is set about 3½ hours before US markets close, so a move in the US afternoon shows up in copper only the next day. That makes copper's daily correlation with both COPX and the S&P 500, and the COPX-per-copper ratio, lower than the true link; COPX and the S&P 500 close at the same time and are not affected. Only days on which both markets traded are used. COPJ (Sprott Junior Copper Miners ETF, Nasdaq closes) is shown with its distributions reinvested, because it paid unusually large year-end distributions ($2.15 per share in December 2024 and $4.55 in December 2025, about 10–12% of its price) that would otherwise appear as one-day crashes; all other lines are price only, and COPX's own distributions are about 2% a year. Copper history starts in January 2008, COPX in October 2016 and COPJ in February 2023, so the longer periods show fewer lines. Every period ends on the latest close both have.

Correlation table and how to read it
How closely they moved togetherValueWhat it means

Correlation here compares monthly moves: +1 means they moved in the same direction every month, 0 means no relation, −1 means opposite directions. The URA ratio says how many percent URA typically moved for each 1% monthly move in uranium. A year has only 12 monthly moves, so for periods under two years the numbers are a rough guide. Uranium is Cameco's month-end spot price (the average of the UxC and TradeTech month-end spot prices, US$ per lb U3O8). Daily spot prices are a paid UxC and TradeTech product, and the free daily quotes (futures and CFDs) trade too thinly to compare day by day. URA (Global X Uranium ETF) is its closing price on the last trading day of each month (Nasdaq historical data, checked against stockanalysis.com), shown with its distributions reinvested: it paid $1.31 per share in December 2021, $1.68 in December 2023 and $2.08 in December 2025, about 5–6% of its price each time, which a price-only line would miss. The adjusted values match stockanalysis.com's adjusted closes to within 0.1%. URA holds uranium miners and other companies in the nuclear fuel chain, so it is not a pure uranium play. The S&P 500 is the index close on the last trading day of each month, price only. URA history here starts in October 2016, so the 20-year view shows only uranium and the S&P 500. The chart ends at the latest month-end Cameco has published, usually early in the following month.

Sources and reliability

Where each price comes from and how far to trust it
Metal What is quoted Source Grade Updates Latest price
  • Gold and silver history (since 1975 and 1970) is stored with the page. New closes are added by the daily run.
  • Copper and nickel are the LME official cash settlements, the price physical contracts settle against. They publish once per London trading day, so the latest date is usually yesterday.
  • Uranium history is Cameco's month-end spot (the average of UxC and TradeTech). Daily points come from Trading Economics. The long-term contract price is shown because utilities buy most uranium on term contracts.
  • Molybdenum, manganese ore and neodymium are Chinese market prices via Trading Economics. On this copy the molybdenum price stays in CNY/kg (the US$/lb conversion on the original needs a live USD/CNY rate); it is the Chinese level, not the Western molybdenum-oxide benchmark (Platts/Fastmarkets), which is paywalled. The Western benchmarks (Platts, Fastmarkets, Argus) are behind paywalls. Their charts start today and add a point with each daily run.
  • Neodymium is a proxy. The covered rare-earth companies sell NdPr oxide, and Ucore and Viridis also depend on dysprosium and terbium, which have no free daily source.
  • Antimony has no free benchmark. The price shown is a dealer's retail price for investors. Use it for direction, not level.
  • Max goes back as far as each free source allows: gold to 1975, silver to 1970, uranium to 1988 (monthly), copper and nickel to 2008. Long-run moves are therefore only comparable within one metal, not across metals.
  • Nickel has a gap from 8 to 25 March 2022. The LME suspended nickel trading after the short squeeze, and the source only shows placeholder values for those days.
  • SIL (Global X Silver Miners) and SILJ (Amplify Junior Silver Miners) use Nasdaq's historical closes since October 2016; they match stockanalysis.com and digrin.com on every checked day and month-end. Placeholder rows in the Nasdaq files (20 Apr 2026 in both, 29–30 Jan 2024 in SILJ) were replaced with the stockanalysis close where one exists and otherwise left out. New days are added by the daily run.
  • COPJ (Sprott Junior Copper Miners ETF) uses Nasdaq's daily closes since February 2023, adjusted for its three year-end distributions (Dec 2023 $0.46, Dec 2024 $2.15, Dec 2025 $4.55 per share) as if they were reinvested; the adjusted values match stockanalysis.com's adjusted closes. New days are added by the daily run.
  • COPX (Global X Copper Miners ETF) uses Nasdaq's historical closes since October 2016; they match stockanalysis.com on 8 checked days and digrin.com on 9 month-ends. New days are added by the daily run.
  • NIKL (Sprott Nickel Miners ETF) uses Nasdaq's daily closes since its launch on 22 March 2023, adjusted for its three December distributions ($2.77 in 2023, $0.37 in 2024, $0.40 in 2025) as if they were reinvested. The closes match a second download (StockScan) on all 21 overlapping days and stockanalysis.com on 8 checked days, and the adjusted values match stockanalysis.com's adjusted closes. New days are added by the daily run.
  • URA (Global X Uranium ETF) uses Nasdaq's daily closes since October 2016, adjusted for all 15 distributions since then as if they were reinvested (amounts from stockanalysis.com, matching digrin.com and dividendhistory.net). The closes match stockanalysis.com on all 53 checked days. The uranium comparison chart uses month-ends because the free uranium spot history is monthly. New days are added by the daily run.
  • The copper-versus-S&P 500 chart uses the same LME copper prices as the copper card and the stored S&P 500 closes, both updated by the daily run.
  • GDXJ (junior gold miners) uses Nasdaq's historical closes since October 2016, checked against digrin.com month-end prices and stockanalysis.com; the 20 Apr 2026 Nasdaq row was a placeholder and was replaced with the stockanalysis close (131.56). New days are added by the daily run.
  • The comparison chart's GDX line uses Nasdaq's historical closes up to June 2024 and stockanalysis.com since July 2024, because the FMP plan doesn't include ETFs. On the 576 days both cover they match to the cent, except 20 Apr 2026, where the Nasdaq file holds a placeholder (no volume, previous day's price); the stockanalysis close is used there. New days are added by the daily run.
  • Charts spanning many years plot a thinned series (at most about 700 points) so they stay fast. Latest prices and changes always use the full data.
  • The freshness dot turns amber after 5 days without a new price and red after 10.
