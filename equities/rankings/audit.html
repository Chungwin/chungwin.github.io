What the audit found, and what was fixed

Every night an automated update re-reads the companies' news and prices and re-scores the rankings. After every update, a separate check goes over that work. It reads stock-exchange filings and newswires, not the company websites the update used, and looks for wrong figures and for releases the update missed. The check only reports; it never changes a page. The next nightly update fixes what it found, and the audit after that confirms the fix. Every correction is listed below with the correct figure and its source.

Judgement questions the owner decided

Some findings are not errors of fact but questions of judgement: an odds call that no longer fits the numbers, a ranking that contradicts its own scores, an entry-plan step whose trigger has already passed. These go to the owner, and nothing changes until he decides. Each decision is listed with its reason, whether the call was kept or changed.

No decided judgement questions yet. When the audit questions a ranking, an odds call or an entry plan, the owner decides, and the decision and its reason appear here.

How the audit works

Daily check

Tuesday to Saturday, about 10:00 Hamburg time, after each nightly update

Every figure, odds change and news item the night's update changed, checked against the filing or the newswire copy. Every company is checked for releases from the last two days that the update missed. A fifth of the share prices is checked each day, so every price is checked once a week.

Weekly check

Sunday, about 12:00 Hamburg time

Every company's share count, cash, debt and latest quarterly figures against its latest filing. Every release of the past seven days. Everything Saturday's weekly review changed.

Evaluation audit

First Sunday of each month, plus once after each results season

Do the rankings, odds and entry plans still make sense? It looks for dates that have passed, triggers already hit, reasons that no longer fit the numbers and rankings that contradict their own scores, and it writes the bear case for the top names. It runs no more often than this, so the evaluations do not churn.

  • Severity. serious a figure that feeds the odds, a rank, an entry plan or a valuation (share count, cash, debt, a study's value or cost, the price the odds were set at) that is off by more than 10%, or a missed release that changes the odds or a plan. normal a wrong figure or a missed release that changes no conclusion. minor a stale date, rounding or wording.
  • What is not published. Findings the audit withdrew after re-checking, and judgement questions the owner has not decided yet. Everything else is here.
  • Limits. The audit is a second pair of eyes, not a guarantee. It is automated, it can miss things, and some filings cannot be read automatically (Canada's SEDAR+ often blocks it), so it then falls back to the newswire copy of a release.
