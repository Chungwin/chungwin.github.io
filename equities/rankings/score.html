Was the judgement any good?

Every company's odds, rank and risk on the day it was first scored, against today. The rankings make a claim: names above the benchmark line should multiply more often than names below it, and the safety list's top should hold up better than its bottom. This page is where that claim gets checked, in public, whether or not it flatters the method.

Read the numbers for what they are. The record is 51 days old; the rankings are built for a 3–5 year horizon. Thirty days of market-cap moves say almost nothing about whether a 15% odds call was right — a junior can halve on a quiet week and triple on one drill hole. Until the record covers at least a year, and events have resolved (studies delivered, permits granted or refused, financings closed), the tables below are a ledger, not a verdict. Market cap is used as the yardstick because the daily snapshots did not store share prices before 1 Nov 2026; it overstates a company's performance after a share issue. Share-price change is shown where both ends are on record.
